Cinatra — Application Design

Cinatra Application Design — the canonical reference for the interface: colour, type, and components. It applies to the Cinatra app and to every extension that ships a UI; the web surfaces — corporate site, marketplace, docs — build on it through their own specs. Everything in this document is normative; match it exactly. The Cinatra name and marks are trademarks; the mark and wordmark themselves are governed by the Cinatra brand guidelines.

VI. Rules of the road

Editorial · 8 do's
1. Cream rules below the brand
The product is set on cream. Don't introduce alternative greys, pure blacks, or off-whites for surfaces. The five surface tokens, plus the toolbar chrome ramp, are the entire system.
2. Navy carries the text, indigo carries the action
All primary text is #15213A. Captions use slate #5A6477. Filled primary buttons, focus rings, links, and the "running" status pill use indigo #364E81. Same hue family as the navy, so action lifts off the text without clashing.
3. Mustard is for the brand and "needs you"
The wordmark, the marquee bulbs, the On-hold chip, a "needs you" badge, warning intent (callouts, alerts, toasts), and the Context "Updating" spinner are the only places mustard appears. It is the brand colour, so it is never an extension-type ground, a body colour, or a regular button.
4. Red is for stop and destroy
Reserved for destructive buttons (Decline / Tear stub) and for terminal failures. Red does not mean "running" — indigo does. Don't introduce a third red. An extension type may still draw red as a categorical ground — there the colour is identity, not alarm.
5. One sea-green for approval
#3F6E6B is the only green in the system. Don't introduce mint, Slack-green, or "success" greens — they will fight with the tweed feel. It carries the "approved" meaning everywhere except as an extension ground, where it's simply one of the eight categorical colours.
6. Hairlines are navy, not grey
All hairlines use navy at low alpha. Major section dividers use full navy as paired rules — the etched-glass treatment. Never use a neutral grey on a divider.
7. Emblems identify, colour adds variety
Each extension carries a fixed emblem (type icon, or vendor logo for Connectors) and a live indicator on the right. The background ground is drawn at random from the seven categorical accents — burgundy, red, sea-green, rust, olive, aubergine and clay — at creation. Mustard is never used: it is the brand colour. Indigo and navy stay out too (action and text). Two Agents will rarely share a colour. Brand logos and status indicators always sit in a white pill — never a black or navy badge — so they read against any coloured ground.
8. White means interactive
Pure-white --surface-strong (#FFFFFF) is reserved for elements the user actually touches — text inputs, clickable cards, popovers, dropdown menus. Everything else (presentation cards, panels, stat tiles, palette swatches) sits on the warm-cream --surface (#F7F7F3). The eye learns to seek the brighter surface when input is invited.

VII. Content widths

7 roles · Tailwind scale · rem / px

The app is a fixed 16rem sidebar beside a fluid content stage. Nothing inside that stage runs edge-to-edge: every page holds its content to one of a small set of max-widths, each centred in the stage, each a named step on Tailwind's max-w-* scale. The rule mirrors the one for text (VIII) — pick the narrowest width the content tolerates: reading stays comfortable, forms stay scannable, and one page never sprawls while the next feels cramped.

The stage & its gutters

A fixed 16rem (256px) sidebar — 3rem collapsed — sits beside a fluid content stage; every content width above is a max-width centred (mx-auto) inside that stage — page content below the top bar is never edge-anchored. Content carries equal left and right gutters — px-5 → sm:px-8 (20 → 32px) — so it is symmetric at every viewport: pure gutter below its cap, growing side margins above it. The top-bar is chrome, not content: its header is full-bleed and its control row spans the full available width — the viewport minus the persistent sidebar (the sidebar's inner edge is the left bound when it is shown; otherwise viewport x=0) — carrying only the standard edge gutters (px-5 → sm:px-8). So the top-bar's left element sits at the far-left edge and its right element at the far-right edge, independent of the max-w-7xl content cap. The breadcrumb is the top-bar's left element and moves with the bar — it anchors at that far-left edge; it does not ride the content stage. Only the content below the top bar stays capped and centred on the max-w-* stage.

8. Callouts

When a paragraph needs to be lifted out of the reading flow — a note, a caveat, a warning — set it as a callout. Callouts borrow the status-pill construction at block scale: a tinted ground, a hairline border one step stronger, and a mono uppercase label led by its icon. There is no left accent bar; the label row carries the semantics. Reserve them for genuine asides — a page wall-to-wall with callouts has none.

IX. Approvals inbox

1 surface · 2 directions · 4 states

The unified approvals surface at Configuration → Approvals. One federated page gathers every approval family — agent creation requests and the marketplace moderation queues — behind two direction tabs. Each family stays authoritative: the page reads from a source registry and every decision executes at its own source, so a section can be added or removed without touching the page. Sections stream independently — a slow or failing source never blocks its siblings.

Details

The page opens with the standard page header (title Approvals, one-line description, closing etched rule) and two direction tabs — Inbox (requests from others awaiting the viewer's decision) and Your requests (requests the viewer submitted). Underneath, one section per source: a 13px section header, an optional View all drill-down link on the right, and a card whose rows are hairline-divided (the last row drops its divider). Every row is a title line — linked identity + a status pill + any flags — over a muted meta line, with the action slot right-aligned. Sources render in a fixed registry order — Inbox: agent creation requests · workflow approvals · extension submissions · vendor applications; Your requests: agent creation requests · this instance's submissions · this instance's vendor application. A request the viewer authored is a Your requests item only — it never appears in the Inbox, and it contributes no badge and no row there, even when the viewer is the only administrator on the instance. Its own pending row lives under Your requests, and where the viewer is permitted to clear their own request — the sole platform administrator, or an instance carrying the allowSelfApproval configuration — the approve / publish control renders on that own pending row. That own row is where a sole administrator publishes the agent they created through /chat. The Inbox therefore only ever holds requests that someone else must decide.

Chat-created agents are always pending

An agent creation initiated from /chat is always recorded as a pending request — never instant-published — regardless of the author's role, up to and including a platform administrator and the sole administrator on the instance. This is the ratified deliberate-publish behavior: the conversational surface never grants an agent live in a single step, so a reviewable request always exists and the publish is always an explicit act. An instant grant — an agent created already live, with no pending step — exists only on the dedicated non-chat authoring surface, and only for a platform administrator. A sole administrator therefore still sees their own /chat creation as a pending row under Your requests and publishes it there with that row's approve / publish control (above) — never silently, and never from the Inbox.

Direction tabs & count pills

The tabs use the standard underline treatment (see Tabs) and drive the shipped URL contract (?direction=inbox · ?direction=mine). Each carries a count pill of the actionable items in that direction, summed across sources: the Inbox pill is the indigo default Badge, Your requests the muted one. A direction pill is hidden at zero and caps its label at 99+. Remote (marketplace) sources report a capped count — a moderator only needs to know items exist, not the exact backlog — so a remote-only section reads at most 9+. On load the page lands on the populated direction, preferring Inbox; legacy deep links resolve into Inbox anchored to their section.

Marketplace connectivity — four states

The four marketplace sources share one group. Connectivity is decided locally (credential presence only, no network) so a disconnected instance fires zero remote calls. Four states — not connected and zero items use the design-system Empty family; not configured is a footer hint and section error is an inline destructive card:

(a) Not connected — no marketplace credential of any kind resolves: the whole group collapses to one Empty with a Connect registry button, and no remote call is made. (b) Not configured — another marketplace credential is connected but this section's own is absent: the section is hidden and a single discoverable footer line names it, so a misconfiguration is never silently masked. (c) Section error — a source load failed: an inline error card with a Try again link, isolated to that section. (d) Zero items — connected and configured but nothing pending: the standard Empty. Local sources (agent creation requests) sit outside the group and always render regardless of marketplace connectivity.

Actions

Decisions execute inline at the row. Approve is the primary button; Reject (and Withdraw, on your own pending submissions) is the outline button, and Reject reveals a required reason before it confirms. An action is either locally enforced — eligibility is knowable up front, so the button state is authoritative (agent creation requests) — or action-time enforced, where only the source can decide at submit time (the marketplace queues); the button shows optimistically and the source's refusal is surfaced in place. When a source supplies row-level eligibility metadata, the matching button is disabled and annotated with its reason; action-time enforcement at the source stays authoritative either way. A read-only row (a vendor application whose lifecycle lives elsewhere) carries a Manage link instead of buttons.

Status vocabulary

Each source maps its native status token onto the four Badge variants. A submission carries two axes — the decision (pending / approved / rejected) drives the pill; the promotion sub-state (queued / promoted / …) is appended to the meta line, never a second pill.

Separation-of-duties refusal

When a source refuses a decision because the actor may not approve their own request, the raw error is replaced with a readable in-place explanation next to the row's actions — never a stack trace or a bare HTTP code. Only genuinely transient failures are marked retryable.

Refused

Someone else must review this — the marketplace does not allow the submitter or namespace owner to approve their own request.

The same treatment covers a state conflict (the row was already decided elsewhere) and a missing capability on the instance's credential — each rendered as its own readable line rather than a generic failure.
